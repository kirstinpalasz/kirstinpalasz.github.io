Sweetgreen Analysis & Growth Strategy

Strategic Management · Team project
Full strategic analysis of a public company with a five-year growth plan
StrategyCompetitive analysisFinancial modelingMarket entry

For my Strategic Management course, our team ran a full strategic analysis of Sweetgreen, the publicly traded fast-casual chain, and turned it into a growth strategy. We worked through external and internal frameworks (STEEPG, Porter's Five Forces, a value chain, and a VRIST resource review) and benchmarked Sweetgreen against Chipotle, CAVA, and Shake Shack on cost structure, margins, and same-store sales. The core finding was real, hard-to-copy advantages, its Infinite Kitchen automation and farm network, sitting inside a cost structure that still runs at a loss. From there we proposed two growth moves, a smart-fridge retail network and a United Kingdom market entry, each backed by a five-year financial model with revenue, margin, and rollout targets. My focus on the team was the smart-fridge concept: a network of premium, data-driven Sweetgreen fridges in airports, gyms, and offices, scaling from a 50-unit pilot to a 1,200-fridge fleet across 30 markets by 2030, with a target of roughly $30M in revenue at about a 12% operating margin.

Red Bull Strategic Marketing Plan

Marketing · Team project
Consumer research behind a go-to-market plan for a new product
Consumer researchPersonasProduct launchGo-to-market

A strategic marketing plan for Red Bull built by a four-person team. We started from the consumer: a 150-response survey and two data-backed personas, a content-creating outdoor athlete and a multitasking student, that surfaced a clear gap, heavy energy-drink users want a portable, discreet, spill-free format the can cannot deliver. From there we proposed a new product, Red Bull Boost Bites, bite-sized energy chews, and built a three-year go-to-market plan across digital, sampling, and retail distribution, with launch goals and KPIs at each stage.

Bojangles Game-Day Partnership Pitch

Sports Business · Team project
A Gen Z growth strategy with three activation concepts and a measurement plan
Sports marketingConsumer strategyGen ZMeasurement

A strategic partnership pitch for Bojangles and Tepper Sports & Entertainment, the group behind the Carolina Panthers and Charlotte FC. Our team, Bo-Tato, took on a real commercial problem: a beloved Southern brand with an aging customer base that was not reaching Gen Z. We built the case around how younger fans actually behave on game day, watching, posting, and ordering all at once, then proposed three integrated activations: a creator content competition, real-time in-app offers tied to live game moments, and a gamified prediction game for loyalty. My focus on the team was idea development and execution, shaping the activation concepts and how they would actually come to life.

Point of view

Analytics has moved from the back office to the front line of how businesses and teams compete, and the edge now goes to whoever turns data into a decision.

01

Decision speed is the advantage. In sports and in business, the winners are increasingly the organizations that convert data into faster, sharper calls on talent, pricing, fans, and strategy, not the ones that simply collect the most of it.

02

Translation is the scarce skill. Models are everywhere; people who can connect the numbers to human behavior and a commercial goal are not. That intersection of behavioral insight, business context, and analytical rigor is where I work, and it is what my Red Bull and Sweetgreen projects were really about.

03

Sports is now a data business. From player tracking and athlete health to ticketing, fan engagement, and the rapid growth of legal sports betting across the US, data sits at the center of how modern sports organizations compete and grow. It is the market I most want to build my career in.

04

Fan data is becoming the asset. Teams are increasingly using first-party fan data to inform ticket pricing, sponsorship valuation, personalized marketing, and retention. That intersection of behavior, commercial strategy, and analytics is exactly where I want to work.
